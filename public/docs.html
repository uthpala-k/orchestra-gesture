GESTURE-CONTROLLED CINEMATIC MUSIC IN THE BROWSER

Orchestra / Gesture

A two-hand performance instrument: conduct orchestral harmony with the left hand and play expressive solo voices with the right hand.

Documentation version 1.1 · August 2026

Creator: Uthpala Kaushalya · independent freelance / fun project. Interaction concept influenced by gesture.live; no affiliation.

2. Start the app

  1. Open the app in a current Chrome or Edge browser.
  2. Press START CAMERA + AUDIO.
  3. Allow camera access.
  4. Wait until the top status says the orchestra and solo samples are ready.
  5. Keep both hands comfortably inside the camera frame.
Browsers require a user gesture before Web Audio can start. That is why the Start button is required.

Local development

npm install npm run import:samples npm run install:solo-samples npm run dev

The application uses a true 16:9 mirrored camera performance area, with most controls compressed into the bottom strip.

4. Left hand — orchestra

The physical left hand controls harmony. Keep it visible while a chord should continue. A fist or removing the entire left hand from the camera releases the chord.

The app waits briefly for a hand shape to stabilize so a three-finger gesture does not accidentally trigger I and II while the fingers are being raised.

6. Palm / back — harmonic polarity

With the default automatic behavior:

  • Palm facing camera: selected scale/mode.
  • Back of hand facing camera: parallel opposite major/minor family.

Examples: Major ↔ Minor. Dorian / Phrygian / melodic minor / harmonic minor are treated as minor-family modes; Lydian / Mixolydian are treated as major-family modes.

If your camera identifies the surfaces backwards, open Settings → Chords → Swap palm/back detector.

7. Right hand — soloist

The right-hand center height sets pitch. Pinching the thumb to a finger activates that finger's assigned instrument.

PinchDefault voice
Thumb + indexViolin
Thumb + middleCello
Thumb + ringPiano
Thumb + pinkyFlute

Open Settings → Melody to assign instruments. Available choices include violin, viola, cello, piano, flute, clarinet, oboe, French horn, saxophone variants and synthetic voices.

8. Snap vs Glide

SNAP

The right hand moves through 14 wide scale-note lanes. Only scale notes are allocated lanes, so each note receives more physical vertical space. Hysteresis means a tiny hand wobble does not immediately change notes.

Gold lanes indicate pitch classes in the active chord. The other highlighted lanes belong to the selected scale.

GLIDE / THEREMIN

Pitch is continuous instead of quantized. A pinch must remain active for sound to continue.

9. Keys & scales

Keys: C, C#/Db, D, Eb, E, F, F#/Gb, G, Ab, A, Bb and B.

Scales/modes: Major, Minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian, Phrygian, Melodic Minor and Harmonic Minor.

The current scale determines both the harmony engine and the right-hand snapped pitch lanes.

Global mix trims

Settings → Sound contains independent Orchestra Mix and Solo Mix sliders. These are mixer trims; they do not remove the expressive hand-dynamics mapping.

V1.1 defaults to a stronger orchestra and uses additional section makeup gain plus a master limiter.

11. Record, preview, trim and download

  1. Press RECORD.
  2. A full-screen 3 → 2 → 1 countdown appears. The file is not recording during the countdown.
  3. Recording starts after 1 disappears. The mirrored camera, hand skeleton, HUD and generated audio are captured together.
  4. Press STOP RECORDING.
  5. The Recording Review editor opens automatically.
  6. Preview the video with normal video controls.
  7. Move the Start and End sliders or position the playhead and press SET IN TO PLAYHEAD / SET OUT TO PLAYHEAD.
  8. Press PREVIEW SELECTION to watch only the chosen range.
  9. Choose DOWNLOAD FULL or EXPORT TRIMMED MP4.
The trimmed MP4 is created locally in the browser. Accurate trimming re-encodes the selected range, so long recordings may take some time. A progress indicator is shown.

12. Pastoral / folk voices — V1.1

V1.1 adds a group of pastoral folk colours designed for gentle fantasy, Celtic-adjacent and acoustic-style improvisation. These voices do not reproduce any copyrighted soundtrack melody or recording.

VoiceCharacter
WhistleSampled whistle colour for bright pastoral leads.
Pan fluteReal sampled pan-flute timbre.
RecorderReal sampled recorder.
OcarinaReal sampled ocarina.
Celtic / folk harpReal sampled orchestral harp used as the folk-harp colour.
Hammered dulcimerReal sampled dulcimer.
Musette / folk accordionReal sampled accordion used as the musette/folk reed colour.

These V1.1 voices use real sampled instruments from FluidR3_GM through the open midi-js-soundfonts distribution (CC BY 3.0). They are level-matched to sit near the processed solo instruments.

Per-finger octave, volume and reverb

Settings → Melody gives each of the four pinch channels its own Octave −/+ control from −2 to +2 octaves. The octave setting belongs to that finger slot, so the four simultaneously assigned instruments can sit in different registers.

Whistle, Pan Flute, Recorder and Ocarina default to +1 octave because their higher register sounds more natural in the shared melody-lane system. Selecting another instrument resets that slot to the recommended default, and the performer can immediately change it with the −/+ buttons.

The same Melody row also provides a Volume slider (0–150%) and a Reverb slider (0–100%). Volume and reverb are stored per voice. Reverb is additional to the broader hall-space control available from the left-hand roll.

Whistle, Pan Flute, Recorder, Ocarina and Musette are finite real recordings, so V1.1 refreshes the real sample while a SNAP pinch remains held. These sustaining voices therefore continue sounding until the pinch is released. Plucked voices such as harp and dulcimer are intentionally allowed to decay naturally.

13. Orchestral Pulse — V1.1

The BPM control is now functional through the new Orchestral Pulse performance mode. Sustain mode keeps the original continuous chord. Pulse mode turns the currently displayed left-hand chord into an original algorithmic accompaniment pattern.

MeterPulse grouping
4/42+2+2+2 eighth-note cells
2/42+2
3/4Waltz-style three-beat cycle
6/83+3 pastoral compound pulse
5/82+3
7/82+2+3

The accompaniment follows chord changes in real time, uses the selected BPM, and stops on a fist or when the left hand leaves the camera. V1.1 uses short VSCO orchestral samples (staccato/spiccato/pizzicato where available), harp and timpani rather than synthesized beep voices. No melody or audio from a film soundtrack is included.

14. Settings reference

Chords

Key, scale, palm/back policy, palm/back inversion and seven-chord overview.

Melody

Assign a voice to each right-hand pinch channel, adjust that finger's octave from −2 to +2 with the −/+ buttons, choose Snap/Glide mode, and set per-voice Volume (0–150%) and Reverb. Whistle, Pan Flute, Recorder and Ocarina start at +1 octave. Naturally sustaining sampled folk winds/reeds are refreshed while a SNAP pinch remains held; plucked instruments such as harp and dulcimer decay naturally.

Sound

Orchestra mix and Solo mix are final output faders. V1.1 defaults to Orchestra 15% and Solo 70%. These controls change the final bus levels without changing the hand-expression mapping.

Camera

720p wide mode, 1080p mode, browser auto mode and hand-tracking rate (18 / 24 / 30 fps).

Recording

Start/stop recording and reopen the most recent recording review.

About

Creator information, social links, V1.1 feature summary, credits, licenses, attribution requirements and a link back to this guide.

VSCO 2 Community Edition by Versilian Studios. The project uses imported orchestral WAV samples for ensemble sections. VSCO 2 CE is CC0/public-domain licensed.

Solo

The optional processed solo library comes from nbrosowsky/tonejs-instruments. The samples are CC BY 3.0 and are preferred for supported solo voices because the collection has been processed for browser instrument use.

When deploying processed solo samples, keep public/solo-samples/ATTRIBUTION.txt.

16. Performance tips

  • Keep hands at a comfortable distance; do not push them close to the lens.
  • Use horizontal motion—not camera distance—for dynamics.
  • Keep fingers clearly separated for chord gestures.
  • Move the right hand smoothly through the wide note lanes.
  • If tracking is sluggish, choose 18 or 24 fps in Camera Settings.
  • Use Settings → Sound to balance the global orchestra/solo relationship before performing.

18. Technical architecture

Webcam ↓ MediaPipe Hand Landmarker ↓ Gesture engine ├─ Left: degree / extension / surface / dynamics / roll └─ Right: pinch / pitch / dynamics / vibrato ↓ Music theory + orchestration engine ↓ Tone.js / Web Audio ↓ Canvas HUD + audio bus ↓ Mediabunny ├─ live MP4 recording └─ browser-local trim conversion

19. Privacy

Hand tracking and audio generation run in the browser. The webcam frame is used by the hand-tracking model to generate landmarks. Recording is created locally in the browser and is not uploaded by this application merely to create or trim the MP4.
